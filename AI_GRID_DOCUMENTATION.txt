SECTION 1: BASE PAPERS, STANDARDS & ARCHITECTURAL FOUNDATIONS
================================================================================

1. MAIN BASE PAPER:
   Title    : "QB4AIRA: A Question Bank for Responsible AI Risk Assessment"
   Authors  : Sung Une Lee, Qinghua Lu, Liming Zhu, Xiwei Xu, et al.
   Institute: CSIRO Data61, Australia
   Journal  : IEEE Software, Volume 42, Issue 3, Pages 115-124, 2024
   DOI      : https://doi.org/10.1109/MS.2024.3512577
   Free ArXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09300

   SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
   This is the main research paper our project is built on. A team of 
   researchers from Australia created a "Question Bank" — basically a 
   structured list of important questions you should ask before deploying 
   any AI system. These questions cover things like: Is the AI fair? Is it 
   safe? Can people understand how it makes decisions? Our AI-GRID tool 
   takes these questions and turns them into an automated scoring system.

2. SUPPORTING PAPER 1 (AI Governance & Taxonomy):
   Title    : "An AI Harms and Governance Framework for Trustworthy AI"
   Authors  : Jeremy Peckham
   Journal  : IEEE Computer, Volume 57, Issue 3, Pages 59-68, March 2024
   DOI      : https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.2024.3354040

   SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
   This paper classifies the different types of harm AI can cause (privacy 
   violations, discrimination, physical harm, etc.) and proposes governance 
   frameworks to prevent them. We use it to structure our 7 trust dimensions.

3. SUPPORTING PAPER 2 (Bottleneck & Assurance Adequacy):
   Title    : "Risk-Based AI Assurance Framework (RBAAF)"
   Authors  : Aoun E. Muhammad, Kin-Choong Yow
   Institute: University of Regina, Canada
   Journal  : MDPI Information, 2024

   SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
   This paper introduces the idea that an AI system is only as trustworthy 
   as its WEAKEST dimension. For example, if an AI scores 95% on transparency 
   but only 20% on safety, it's still dangerous. This "bottleneck principle" 
   is the foundation of our scoring algorithm — the weakest dimension drags 
   down the overall trust score.

4. CORE REGULATORY & TECHNICAL STANDARDS:
   - Model Context Protocol (MCP) 2.0 Specification (Anthropic / Linux Foundation, 2024-2026)
   - EU AI Act: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and Council
     * Focused Articles: 9 (Risk Management), 10 (Data Governance), 
       13 (Transparency), 14 (Human Oversight), 15 (Cybersecurity & Robustness)
   - NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, NIST SP 1270)
     * 4 Core Lifecycle Functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE
   - FAIR Model: Factor Analysis of Information Risk (Open Group Standard)
   - IEEE P8000.1: Standard for Method & Criteria to Assess Trustworthiness of AI

   SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
   These are the laws and standards we check AI systems against:
   - EU AI Act = Europe's law that classifies AI into risk tiers (Minimal, 
     Limited, High, Unacceptable) and bans the most dangerous ones.
   - NIST AI RMF = America's framework for managing AI risks through 4 steps: 
     Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.
   - MCP = The protocol that modern AI agents use to connect to external tools 
     (databases, APIs, sensors). AI-GRID probes this connection for security holes.

SECTION 2: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PRECISE PROJECT SCOPE
================================================================================

PROJECT TITLE:
AI-GRID v2.0: Automated Security Scanner, Empirical Trust Evaluator, and 
Regulatory Compliance Pre-Audit Engine for Tool-Augmented AI and Agentic 
MCP Systems — with Dual-Mode Assessment (Questionnaire + MCP Live Audit)

WHAT IS AI-GRID? (SIMPLE EXPLANATION):
Imagine you build an AI system — maybe a medical diagnosis tool, a loan 
approval system, or a self-driving car component. Before deploying it to 
real users, you need to answer critical questions:
  - Is it fair to all demographics? (Fairness)
  - Can people understand its decisions? (Transparency)
  - Does it handle bad inputs safely? (Safety)
  - Does it protect user data? (Privacy)
  - Is a human reviewing its critical decisions? (Accountability)
  - Can hackers trick it with fake data? (Security)
  - Could it cause harm to society or the environment? (Societal Impact)

AI-GRID automatically evaluates ALL 7 of these dimensions, gives you a 
trust score (0-100), tells you which EU law category your AI falls into, 
and generates a prioritized fix-it list.

DUAL-MODE ARCHITECTURE (NEW IN v2.0):
AI-GRID v2.0 supports TWO ways to assess any AI system:

  MODE 1: QUESTIONNAIRE-BASED ASSESSMENT (No MCP Required)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  - The user answers 25 structured questions about their AI system across 
    6 categories (Model Identity, Training Data, Safety, Transparency, 
    Privacy, Accountability).
  - AI-GRID computes all 7 trust dimension scores from these answers.
  - This mode works for ANY AI system — no technical connection needed.
  - Perfect for: early-stage projects, legacy models, AI systems without 
    MCP interfaces, quick risk assessments.

  MODE 2: MCP LIVE AUDIT (Requires Running MCP Server)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  - AI-GRID connects to the target AI's MCP server over JSON-RPC.
  - It automatically discovers registered tools, probes safety guardrails, 
    tests adversarial robustness, inspects metadata, and extracts evidence.
  - This mode provides deeper, evidence-based assessment.
  - Perfect for: production-deployed AI agents, MCP-compliant systems, 
    agentic AI architectures.

  Both modes produce IDENTICAL output:
  → 7-dimension trust scores
  → Composite AAS (Assurance Adequacy Score)
  → EU AI Act risk tier classification
  → NIST AI RMF compliance mapping
  → Prioritized remediation roadmap
  → What-If deployment scenario simulation

WHAT "ANY AI SYSTEM" MEANS:
- With Questionnaire Mode: literally any AI system in any domain — you 
  just answer questions about it.
- With MCP Mode: any AI system that exposes its capabilities via the 
  Model Context Protocol (MCP). For legacy models (scikit-learn, PyTorch, 
  XGBoost), the "MCP Adapter Pattern" wraps the model inference as an 
  MCP tool declaration.

SECTION 4: THE 25-QUESTION WIZARD (QUESTIONNAIRE MODE)

When the user selects "Answer Questions" mode, AI-GRID presents a 6-step 
wizard with 25 structured questions. Each question maps to one or more of 
the 7 trust dimensions. Here is the complete question breakdown:

STEP 1: MODEL IDENTITY (5 questions)
─────────────────────────────────────
These questions identify WHAT the AI system is.

  Q1.  "What is the name of your AI model/system?"
       → Type: Text input
       → Example: "Customer Churn Predictor", "Medical Imaging Classifier"
       → Purpose: Labels the audit report

  Q2.  "What is the current version?"
       → Type: Text input
       → Example: "1.0.0", "2.3.1"
       → Purpose: Tracks which version was assessed

  Q3.  "What domain does this AI operate in?"
       → Type: Dropdown selection
       → Options: Healthcare, Finance, Agriculture, Education, Autonomous 
         Vehicles, Criminal Justice, Entertainment, E-Commerce, Manufacturing, 
         Cybersecurity, Other
       → Purpose: Domain determines the What-If simulation multiplier and 
         contextual risk level

  Q4.  "What is the model architecture?"
       → Type: Text input
       → Example: "Transformer (GPT-4)", "CNN (ResNet-50)", "Random Forest"
       → Purpose: Documents technical architecture for the audit report

  Q5.  "What is the autonomy level of this AI?"
       → Type: Dropdown selection
       → Options:
         - Advisory Only (human makes the final decision)
         - Semi-Autonomous (AI acts, human can override)
         - Fully Autonomous (AI acts independently)
       → Maps to: Accountability & Oversight dimension
       → Impact: Fully autonomous + no human oversight = severe penalty

────────────────────────────────────

SECTION 5: ALGORITHM SPECIFICATIONS (HOW SCORES ARE CALCULATED)
================================================================================

ALGORITHM 1: QUESTIONNAIRE-BASED SCORING ENGINE
────────────────────────────────────────────────
SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
Each of the 7 trust dimensions starts at 100 points (perfect score). 
For every question where the user selects a weak or missing capability, 
points are deducted. The deduction amount depends on how critical that 
capability is. After all deductions, each dimension is clamped to a 
minimum of 10 (you can't score below 10).

HOW IT WORKS:
  Input : User's 25 questionnaire answers (JSON dictionary)
  Output: 7 dimension scores, each between 10.0 and 100.0

  For each dimension:
    1. Start with base_score = 100.0
    2. For each relevant question:
       - If answer is the "worst" option: apply LARGE penalty (15-45 points)
       - If answer is the "middle" option: apply MODERATE penalty (5-20 points)
       - If answer is the "best" option: no penalty (0 points)
    3. Final_Score = clamp(base_score - total_penalties, 10.0, 100.0)

  PENALTY EXAMPLES:
    - No human oversight for a fully autonomous system → -45 points
    - No explainability engine (black box model) → -45 points
    - No input validation → -30 points
    - No encryption at rest → -30 points
    - Severe class imbalance in training data → -18 points
    - No human-readable reasoning → -10 points

ALGORITHM 1B: MCP-BASED LIVE PROBING ENGINE
───────────────────────────────────────────
SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
When the user has a real MCP server running, AI-GRID connects to it and 
runs automated tests. It discovers what tools the AI has, calls each 
tool with normal and adversarial inputs, and scores the AI based on 
what actually happens (not just what the user says).

HOW IT WORKS:
  Input : MCP Server connection (URL or STDIO command)
  Output: Same 7 dimension scores (10.0 - 100.0)

  Process:
    1. Connect to MCP server via JSON-RPC.
    2. Call `list_tools()` to discover registered capabilities.
    3. Call metadata tool to extract model info, encryption flags, 
       retention policies, oversight requirements.
    4. Call prediction tools with normal inputs vs. adversarial inputs 
       to test fairness, safety guardrails, confidence disparity.
    5. Call explainability tools to verify feature attributions exist 
       and sum correctly.
    6. Call adversarial testing tools to measure robustness.
    7. Compute dimension scores from actual probe observations.

ALGORITHM 2: BOTTLENECK ASSURANCE ADEQUACY SCORE (AAS)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
The AAS is the OVERALL trust score for the AI system. It uses a special 
formula that prevents one really high score from hiding a dangerous low 
score. This is called the "Bottleneck Principle" — the overall score is 
dragged down by the weakest dimension.

FORMULA:
  Weighted_Average = (1/7) × sum(all 7 dimension scores)
  Bottleneck_Score = min(all 7 dimension scores)  ← the weakest one
  
  AAS = (0.60 × Weighted_Average) + (0.40 × Bottleneck_Score)

WHY 0.60 AND 0.40?
  - 60% weight on the average means the overall profile still matters.
  - 40% weight on the weakest dimension means you CAN'T hide a critical 
    failure. If one dimension is terrible (say 20/100), it drags the 
    entire AAS down significantly, even if all other dimensions are 95+.

LETTER GRADES:
  AAS >= 90.0 → Grade A+ (Exceptional Assurance)
  AAS >= 80.0 → Grade A  (Robust Assurance)
  AAS >= 70.0 → Grade B  (Adequate Assurance)
  AAS >= 60.0 → Grade C  (Moderate Risk — Governance Deficits Present)
  AAS >= 50.0 → Grade D  (Elevated Risk — Deployment Should Be Gated)
  AAS <  50.0 → Grade F  (Critical Risk — Unacceptable for Production)

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:
  Suppose scores are: Fairness=67, Transparency=100, Safety=100, 
  Privacy=55, Accountability=35, Security=80, Societal=78

  Weighted_Average = (67+100+100+55+35+80+78) / 7 = 73.6
  Bottleneck_Score = min(35) = 35.0  ← Accountability is the bottleneck
  
  AAS = (0.60 × 73.6) + (0.40 × 35.0) = 44.2 + 14.0 = 58.1
  
  Grade: D (Elevated Risk) — even though 4 dimensions scored 78+, the 
  weak Accountability dimension dragged the overall score to 58.1.

ALGORITHM 3: EU AI ACT RISK TIER CLASSIFICATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────
SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
The EU AI Act (Europe's AI law) classifies AI systems into 4 risk tiers. 
AI-GRID maps the AAS score directly to these tiers so you can see where 
your AI would fall under European law.

MAPPING:
  AAS >= 76.0 → MINIMAL RISK
    Meaning: Low regulatory burden. The AI is considered safe enough 
    that only voluntary codes of conduct apply.

  51.0 <= AAS < 76.0 → LIMITED RISK
    Meaning: Transparency obligations apply. You must disclose to users 
    that they are interacting with AI and show confidence levels.

  26.0 <= AAS < 51.0 → HIGH RISK
    Meaning: Strict regulatory requirements. You need a conformity 
    assessment, risk management system, and mandatory human oversight 
    (Article 14). This is where medical, financial, and criminal justice 
    AI typically falls.

  AAS < 26.0 → UNACCEPTABLE RISK
    Meaning: PROHIBITED from deployment under Article 5. Examples include 
    social scoring systems and real-time biometric surveillance.

ALGORITHM 4: NIST AI RMF 4-FUNCTION LIFECYCLE MAPPING
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
The US government's NIST framework organizes AI risk management into 
4 lifecycle functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE. AI-GRID translates 
the 7 trust dimensions into these 4 functions so you can see compliance 
with American standards too.

MAPPING:
  GOVERN  = (Accountability & Oversight + Privacy & Governance) / 2
            → "Are the right governance structures in place?"
  
  MAP     = (Societal Impact + Fairness & Bias) / 2
            → "Do we understand who is affected and how?"
  
  MEASURE = (Fairness & Bias + Transparency + Safety & Reliability) / 3
            → "Can we quantify the AI's trustworthiness?"
  
  MANAGE  = (Security & Robustness + Safety & Reliability) / 2
            → "Can we control and mitigate ongoing risks?"

STATUS THRESHOLDS:
  Score >= 70.0 → COMPLIANT (green badge)
  Score 45.0 - 69.9 → PARTIALLY COMPLIANT (yellow badge)
  Score < 45.0 → NON-COMPLIANT (red badge)

ALGORITHM 5: WHAT-IF SCENARIO SIMULATION ENGINE
────────────────────────────────────────────────
SIMPLE EXPLANATION:
The same AI system has different risk levels depending on WHERE it's 
deployed and HOW MANY people it affects. A chatbot recommending movies 
is low risk; the same AI making medical diagnoses is high risk. This 
engine lets you simulate: "What if we deployed this AI in healthcare 
instead of entertainment? What if we scaled from 5,000 to 500,000 users?"

FORMULA:
  Base_Risk = 100.0 - Base_AAS

  Domain Multipliers (how much riskier is this domain?):
    - Entertainment / Hobby: 0.8× (lower risk)
    - Education: 1.1×
    - E-Commerce / Retail: 1.1×
    - Manufacturing: 1.2×
    - Finance / Banking: 1.3×
    - Cybersecurity: 1.3×
    - Agriculture: 1.4×
    - Healthcare: 1.5× (higher risk — lives at stake)
    - Autonomous Vehicles: 1.5×
    - Criminal Justice: 1.5×
    - General Purpose: 1.0× (baseline)

  Scale Factor = 1.0 + (min(Users, 1,000,000) / 2,000,000)
    → More users = more exposure = more risk

  Adjusted_Risk = clamp(Base_Risk × Domain_Multiplier × Scale_Factor, 5.0, 99.0)
  Simulated_AAS = 100.0 - Adjusted_Risk

EXAMPLE:
  An AI with AAS = 70.0 (Grade B) is deployed in Healthcare (1.5×) with 
  500,000 users:
    Base_Risk = 100 - 70 = 30
    Scale_Factor = 1.0 + (500000 / 2000000) = 1.25
    Adjusted_Risk = 30 × 1.5 × 1.25 = 56.25
    Simulated_AAS = 100 - 56.25 = 43.75 → HIGH RISK
  
  The same AI that was "Grade B" in a low-risk domain becomes 
  "HIGH RISK" when deployed in healthcare at scale!

SECTION 6: EU AI ACT TECHNICAL PRE-AUDIT SPECIFICATION
================================================================================

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER:
AI-GRID does NOT legally certify AI systems. No software tool can do that —
EU AI Act compliance requires human audits, organizational documentation, 
and review by a Notified Body. AI-GRID acts as an "Automated Technical 
Pre-Audit & Evidence Harvester" that identifies vulnerabilities BEFORE 
formal compliance review.

STRUCTURE: Requirement → Evidence → Test → Result → Risk → Remediation

1. ARTICLE 9 (Risk Management System):
   - Requirement: Continuous identification and mitigation of foreseeable risks.
   - How AI-GRID Tests It: Adversarial robustness testing — probes the model 
     with adversarial noise and corrupted inputs to see if risks are managed.
   - Questionnaire Mapping: Questions about guardrails, adversarial testing, 
     drift monitoring.
   - Score Contribution: Security & Robustness dimension.

2. ARTICLE 10 (Data & Data Governance):
   - Requirement: Training data must be representative across target populations.
   - How AI-GRID Tests It: Checks geographic/demographic coverage ratio, 
     class balance, and known data gaps.
   - Questionnaire Mapping: Questions about dataset size, class balance, 
     coverage percentage, known gaps.
   - Score Contribution: Fairness & Bias dimension.

3. ARTICLE 13 (Transparency & Provision of Information):
   - Requirement: System must provide interpretable explanations and confidence.
   - How AI-GRID Tests It: Checks for explainability engine, feature 
     attributions, human-readable reasoning summaries.
   - Questionnaire Mapping: Questions about SHAP/LIME/Grad-CAM availability, 
     attribution count, reasoning presence.
   - Score Contribution: Transparency dimension.

4. ARTICLE 14 (Human Oversight):
   - Requirement: Enable humans to oversee operation and override decisions.
   - How AI-GRID Tests It: Checks if human-in-the-loop is mandatory, 
     whether incident escalation protocols exist.
   - Questionnaire Mapping: Questions about human oversight level, 
     incident escalation protocol.
   - Score Contribution: Accountability & Oversight dimension.

5. ARTICLE 15 (Accuracy, Robustness, and Cybersecurity):
   - Requirement: Resilience against errors, faults, and adversarial attacks.
   - How AI-GRID Tests It: Input validation coverage, adversarial testing 
     status, encryption protocols, drift monitoring.
   - Questionnaire Mapping: Questions about input validation, encryption 
     at rest/in transit, adversarial testing, drift monitoring.
   - Score Contribution: Safety & Reliability + Security & Robustness.

SECTION 8: VIVA / PANEL DEFENSE SCRIPT (TOP 12 EXAMINER QUESTIONS)
================================================================================

Q1: "Your project claims to audit 'any AI system.' How is that possible?"
A1: "AI-GRID v2.0 supports two modes. In Questionnaire Mode, the user 
     answers 25 structured questions about any AI system — regardless of 
     its architecture, framework, or deployment type. The system computes 
     trust scores from these answers using penalty-based formulas. No 
     technical connection is needed. In MCP Mode, AI-GRID connects to a 
     real MCP server and probes its tools directly. For non-MCP models, 
     we use the 'MCP Adapter Pattern' — a lightweight wrapper that 
     exposes any model's inference as an MCP tool."

Q2: "Where do the scores come from? Are they arbitrary numbers?"
A2: "No. Every score starts at 100 (perfect) and is reduced by 
     deterministic penalty formulas based on concrete evidence. For 
     example, if the user says their model has no human oversight, the 
     Accountability dimension loses exactly 45 points. If data is not 
     encrypted at rest, Privacy loses exactly 30 points. These penalties 
     are derived from the severity weights in the QB4AIRA question bank 
     and the RBAAF assurance framework."

Q3: "What is the difference between Questionnaire Mode and MCP Mode?"
A3: "Questionnaire Mode is based on self-reported answers — the user 
     describes their AI system's characteristics. MCP Mode is evidence-based 
     — AI-GRID actually connects to the AI system and tests it. Both 
     produce the same output format, but MCP Mode provides harder evidence 
     because it observes actual behavior rather than relying on the user's 
     claims. Questionnaire Mode is ideal for early-stage assessment or 
     when MCP is not available."

Q4: "Can AI-GRID legally certify that an AI system is EU AI Act compliant?"
A4: "No software tool can legally certify an AI system, as EU AI Act conformity 
     assessment requires human audits, organizational documentation, and 
     review by a Notified Body. AI-GRID acts as an 'Automated Technical 
     Pre-Audit and Evidence Harvester.' It identifies which risk tier the 
     AI falls into and flags specific Article violations so organizations 
     know what to fix BEFORE the formal audit."

Q5: "Explain the Bottleneck Principle in your scoring algorithm."
A5: "In high-stakes AI governance, a simple arithmetic average is dangerous. 
     A medical AI with 95% transparency and 95% privacy would average 95%, 
     masking a catastrophic 20% safety score. Our formula is:
     AAS = 0.6 × Average + 0.4 × Min(all dimensions). 
     The 40% weight on the weakest dimension means one critical failure 
     drags the entire score down. The overall trust is mathematically 
     constrained by the system's weakest link."

Q6: "Why did you choose the Model Context Protocol (MCP) instead of REST?"
A6: "A REST API tests simple text-in, text-out behavior. MCP standardizes 
     how AI agents declare tools, pass arguments, and handle responses. 
     In autonomous systems, the primary attack surface is at this tool 
     boundary — tool poisoning, argument manipulation, privilege escalation. 
     MCP lets AI-GRID probe these protocol-level vulnerabilities."

Q7: "What is the difference between AI-GRID and tools like IBM AI Fairness 360?"
A7: "IBM AIF360, Google Model Cards, and Microsoft RAI Toolkit are siloed — 
     they address bias, documentation, or interpretability in isolation. 
     AI-GRID is a unified GRC platform that evaluates all 7 trustworthiness 
     dimensions simultaneously, maps them to both EU and US regulations, 
     and provides What-If simulation and remediation roadmaps."

Q8: "How does the What-If Simulation Engine work?"
A8: "It models how risk changes when the same AI is deployed in different 
     contexts. A model with AAS 70 (Grade B) deployed in entertainment is 
     low risk. The same model deployed in healthcare (1.5× multiplier) 
     with 500,000 users (scale factor 1.25) becomes HIGH RISK:
     Adjusted_Risk = 30 × 1.5 × 1.25 = 56.25, Simulated_AAS = 43.75."

Q9: "How do the remediation recommendations work?"
A9: "For every finding, AI-GRID generates: the severity (CRITICAL/HIGH/
     MEDIUM/LOW), which trust dimension is affected, a description of the 
     vulnerability, and a specific, actionable remediation requirement. 
     Findings are sorted by severity so teams fix the most dangerous 
     issues first."

Q10: "Why 25 questions? Why not more or fewer?"
A10: "The 25 questions are mapped directly to the 7 IEEE/QB4AIRA trust 
      dimensions. Each question probes a specific, measurable aspect that 
      has a concrete impact on trust scores. Fewer questions would miss 
      critical dimensions. More would add redundancy without improving 
      scoring accuracy. The 6-step wizard groups questions logically so 
      users understand what each section assesses."

Q11: "What are the limitations of AI-GRID?"
A11: "First, Questionnaire Mode relies on self-reported answers — if the 
      user provides inaccurate information, scores will be wrong. Second, 
      AI-GRID conducts black-box behavioral probing; it does not inspect 
      internal neural network weights. Third, it tests technical and 
      operational controls but cannot evaluate organizational governance 
      policies (such as whistleblower protections). Fourth, the MCP Mode 
      currently requires a running MCP server."

Q12: "How does the system handle edge cases in scoring?"
A12: "All dimension scores are clamped to the range [10.0, 100.0] — you 
      cannot score below 10 even with all worst answers. The AAS formula 
      uses alpha=0.60 as the sensitivity parameter, balancing average 
      performance with bottleneck severity. EU AI Act tiers have clear, 
      non-overlapping boundaries. The simulation engine clamps adjusted 
      risk to [5.0, 99.0] to prevent mathematical impossibilities."
