SECTION 2: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PRECISE PROJECT SCOPE
================================================================================

PROJECT TITLE:
AI-GRID: Automated Security Scanner, Empirical Trust Evaluator, and Regulatory
Compliance Pre-Audit Engine for Tool-Augmented AI and Agentic MCP Systems

PRECISE ARCHITECTURAL SCOPE:
AI-GRID addresses the rapid proliferation of autonomous AI agents and 
tool-augmented AI systems. Unlike legacy static ML models or conversational 
chatbots that operate in text-in/text-out isolation, modern agentic systems 
execute real-world actions by interfacing with external APIs, databases, and 
sensors via standardized protocol layers.

The Model Context Protocol (MCP) has emerged as the open standard mediating 
how AI models declare tools, inspect context, and execute external functions. 
However, MCP introduces a critical attack surface:
  - Tool Poisoning and Shadow Tool execution
  - Parameter and Argument Manipulation (e.g., chemical/financial dosage injection)
  - Privilege Escalation and Broken Tool Authorization
  - Unchecked Automated Actions lacking Human Oversight

AI-GRID provides an automated, wire-level auditing harness that probes target AI 
systems across the MCP boundary. It replaces arbitrary subjective labels with 
empirical, mathematically formulated metrics, harvests verifiable technical 
evidence, evaluates regulatory alignment against the EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF, 
and generates concrete code-level remediation roadmaps.

WHAT "ANY AI SYSTEM" HONESTLY MEANS IN AI-GRID:
AI-GRID targets MCP-compliant AI systems and tool-augmented agents. For legacy 
or traditional ML models (e.g., scikit-learn classifiers, PyTorch/ResNet image 
models, XGBoost tabular estimators), AI-GRID implements the "MCP Adapter Pattern"
—a lightweight microservice wrapper that encapsulates model inference as an MCP 
tool declaration (`predict_soil_and_fertilizer`, `classify_image`, etc.). 
This allows AI-GRID to conduct rigorous black-box behavioral verification without 
requiring access to proprietary model weights.

SECTION 4: EMPIRICAL MEASUREMENT METHODOLOGY FOR THE 6 TRUST DIMENSIONS
================================================================================

AI-GRID rejects subjective score assignments. Every dimension score (0-100) 
is computed dynamically from verifiable probe observations and multi-tier evidence 
using concrete mathematical formulations.

1. SECURITY & ROBUSTNESS:
   - Standards Alignment: EU AI Act Art. 15(4), NIST MEASURE 2.7, OWASP MCP01/07, MITRE ATLAS AML.T0029.
   - Test Procedure:
     * Protocol Probes P02, P04, P07: Type enforcement, dangerous capability inspection,
       and stack trace leakage suppression on malformed JSON-RPC payloads.
     * Software Supply Chain: Scans AI runtime dependencies against NVD/EPSS AI registry.
   - Mathematical Formulation:
       Security Score = 100 - (25 * I(Stack_Leak)) - (25 * I(Dangerous_Shell_Capability))
                            - (15 * I(Malformed_Crash)) - (20 * Mean_Compound_CVE_Risk)
   - Pass Criteria: Score >= 75.0, zero unhandled stack traces, zero undeclared execution primitives.

2. FAIRNESS & NON-DISCRIMINATION:
   - Standards Alignment: EU AI Act Art. 10(2)(f), NIST MEASURE 2.11, EEOC 80% Rule, Kamiran & Calders (2012).
   - Test Procedure:
     * Empirical Evaluation: Evaluates disparate impact on protected demographic cohorts
       (UCI Statlog German Credit benchmark: DIR = 0.896, SPD = -0.075).
     * Coverage Auditing: Identifies underserved cohorts, unbalanced label representations,
       and applies Kamiran & Calders sample reweighing coefficients.
   - Mathematical Formulation:
       DIR = P(Y=1 | Unprivileged) / P(Y=1 | Privileged)
       SPD = P(Y=1 | Unprivileged) - P(Y=1 | Privileged)
       Fairness Score = 100 * [ 0.50 * clamp(DIR / 0.80, 0, 1) + 0.50 * clamp((0.10 - |SPD|) / 0.10, 0, 1) ]

3. TRANSPARENCY & EXPLAINABILITY:
   - Standards Alignment: EU AI Act Art. 13, NIST MAP 1.5, Mitchell et al. (2019).
   - Test Procedure:
     * Protocol Probe P06: Inspects tool metadata, intended purpose, and architectural cards.
     * Verification of prediction-level reasoning summaries, local feature attributions
       (TreeSHAP/LIME), and uncertainty bounds.
   - Mathematical Formulation:
       Transparency Score = 100 - (30 * I(!Feature_Attributions)) - (25 * I(!Model_Card_Published))
                            - (25 * I(!Prediction_Reasoning_Declared)) - (20 * I(!Uncertainty_Bounds))

4. PRIVACY & DATA GOVERNANCE:
   - Standards Alignment: EU AI Act Art. 10(5), GDPR Art. 25/32, NIST GOVERN 1.2, ISO/IEC 42001 §A.7.
   - Test Procedure:
     * Protocol Probe P08: Asserts cryptographic protection (AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit).
     * Audits automated data retention and purging limits (<= 90 days), PII scrubbing,
       and formal data steward designations.
   - Mathematical Formulation:
       Privacy Score = 100 - (30 * I(!At_Rest_Encryption)) - (25 * I(!Transit_TLS13))
                           - (25 * I(Retention_Days > 90)) - (20 * I(!PII_Masking))

5. HUMAN OVERSIGHT & ACCOUNTABILITY:
   - Standards Alignment: EU AI Act Art. 12 & 14, NIST GOVERN 1.4, ACM FAccT (2026).
   - Test Procedure:
     * Validates mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gates on high-stakes/consequential outputs.
     * Checks emergency kill-switch endpoints, 24/7 incident response escalation SLAs,
       and immutable, tamper-evident audit logs.
   - Mathematical Formulation:
       Accountability Score = 100 - (35 * I(!HITL_Approval_Gate)) - (25 * I(!Emergency_Kill_Switch))
                                  - (20 * I(!Escalation_SLA)) - (20 * I(!Tamper_Evident_Logs))

6. SAFETY & RELIABILITY:
   - Standards Alignment: EU AI Act Art. 9 & 15(1), NIST MANAGE 2.2, RBAAF Verification.
   - Test Procedure:
     * Protocol Probes P04 & P05: Injects boundary violations, negative values, and overflows.
     * Asserts deterministic output bounds clamping and verified input rejection rate
       (Clinical Patient Safety benchmark: 100% rejection on corrupted inputs, 28.4% clamping).
   - Mathematical Formulation:
       Safety Score = 100 * [ 0.40 * (Corrupted_Rejected / Corrupted_Total)
                            + 0.35 * (Outputs_Safely_Clamped / Hazardous_Outputs)
                            + 0.25 * (Active_Guardrails >= 3) ]

SECTION 5: ALGORITHM SPECIFICATIONS
================================================================================

ALGORITHM 1: DYNAMIC MCP PROTOCOL PROBING (8 PROTOCOL PROBES)
Input : Target AI MCP Endpoint, Probe Suite P01-P08
Output: Structured Evidence Trace E, Dimension Score Vector S_probe
Process:
  1. Handshake with MCP Server via JSON-RPC 2.0 stdio transport.
  2. P01: Tool Discovery & Enumeration via `list_tools()`.
  3. P02: Schema Validation & Type Safety verification on input_schema declarations.
  4. P03: Permission & Capability Analysis scanning for dangerous execution primitives.
  5. P04: Boundary Testing injecting out-of-range, negative, and overflow values.
  6. P05: Output Validation & Clamping testing output schema conformance and clipping.
  7. P06: Governance Introspection extracting metadata, architecture, and drift tracking.
  8. P07: Error Handling testing malformed payloads for stack trace leakage.
  9. P08: Cryptographic Data Governance checking AES-256, TLS 1.3, and retention limits.
 10. Generate structured Evidence objects (Tier T1, weight 1.00).

ALGORITHM 2: MULTI-TIER EVIDENCE-WEIGHTED BOTTLENECK TRUST SCORING (AAS)
In high-stakes AI governance, subjective questionnaires must be calibrated against 
wire-level protocol observations. Furthermore, naive arithmetic averages allow high 
scores to mask critical safety or security vulnerabilities.

AI-GRID implements the Bottleneck-Penalized Empirical Risk Minimization formula:

  1. Dimension Score with Multi-Tier Evidence Weighting:
     S_d = 100 * [ sum(w_i * s_i * w_evidence,i) / sum(w_i * w_evidence,i) ]
     where w_evidence in {1.0 (T1: Automated Probe), 0.8 (T2: Declared Schema), 0.6 (T3: Self-Report)}

  2. Dimension Confidence:
     C_d = 1.0 - (1.0 / (1.0 + sum(w_evidence,i)))

  3. Bottleneck Assurance Adequacy Score (AAS):
     Weighted_Average = (1/6) * sum(S_d for d in 1..6)
     Bottleneck_Score = min(S_d for d in 1..6)
     AAS = (alpha * Weighted_Average) + ((1.0 - alpha) * Bottleneck_Score)
     (Default alpha = 0.60, with sensitivity curve evaluated across alpha in [0.1, 0.9])

  4. Attestation Discrepancy Index (ADI):
     Delta_d = S_self(d) - S_probe(d)
     ADI = (1/6) * sum(Delta_d)
     Interpretation:
       - ADI > +15.0 : HIGH RISK OF SELF-REPORT OPTIMISM BIAS (Probed reality is far worse)
       - +5.0 <= ADI <= +15.0 : MODERATE DISCREPANCY
       - -5.0 <= ADI < +5.0  : CALIBRATED / HIGH ATTESTATION INTEGRITY
       - ADI < -5.0  : CONSERVATIVE ATTESTATION

Grade Thresholds:
  - AAS >= 90.0: Grade A+ (Exceptional Assurance)
  - AAS >= 80.0: Grade A  (Robust Assurance)
  - AAS >= 70.0: Grade B  (Adequate Assurance)
  - AAS >= 60.0: Grade C  (Moderate Risk - Governance Deficits Present)
  - AAS >= 50.0: Grade D  (Elevated Risk - Deployment Gated)
  - AAS <  50.0: Grade F  (Critical Risk - Unacceptable for Production)

ALGORITHM 3: DOMAIN-BASED EU AI ACT RISK CLASSIFICATION & ARTICLE CONFORMITY
Unlike simplistic score-cutoff methods, AI-GRID classifies systems strictly based on 
their intended purpose under the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689):
  - Article 5 Prohibited: Social scoring, biometric categorization, cognitive manipulation.
  - Annex III High-Risk: Healthcare, biometrics, critical infrastructure, credit/finance, law enforcement.
  - Article 50 Limited Risk: Conversational chatbots, synthetic media, emotion recognition.
  - Minimal Risk: Agronomic advisory, internal inventory, code generators.
Conformity across Articles 9 to 15 is evaluated on an article-by-article basis 
using concrete technical criteria linked to evidence items.

ALGORITHM 4: NIST AI RMF 1.0 4-FUNCTION LIFECYCLE MAPPING
Crosswalks evidence across all 6 dimensions into the 4 core lifecycle functions:
  - GOVERN  = (Human Oversight & Accountability + Privacy & Data Governance) / 2
  - MAP     = (Fairness & Non-Discrimination + Transparency & Explainability) / 2
  - MEASURE = (Fairness & Non-Discrimination + Security & Robustness + Safety & Reliability) / 3
  - MANAGE  = (Security & Robustness + Safety & Reliability) / 2
Status: >= 70.0 (CONFORMANT), 50.0-69.9 (PARTIAL), < 50.0 (NON-CONFORMANT).

ALGORITHM 5: WHAT-IF SCENARIO SIMULATION ENGINE
Predicts risk posture recalculations under deployment shifts and user scaling:
  Domain Multipliers (M_domain):
    - Minimal / Hobbyist: 0.8x
    - Commercial Agriculture: 1.0x
    - Education / Internal: 1.1x
    - Finance / Banking (High-Risk): 1.3x
    - Healthcare / Clinical (High-Risk): 1.5x
    - Autonomous Vehicles / Critical Infra: 1.6x
  User Scale Multiplier: S_users = 1.0 + (min(Users, 1,000,000) / 2,000,000)
  
  Base_Risk = 100.0 - Base_AAS
  Adjusted_Risk = clamp(Base_Risk * M_domain * S_users, 5.0, 99.0)
  Simulated_AAS = 100.0 - Adjusted_Risk

SECTION 8: VIVA / PANEL DEFENSE SCRIPT (TOP 10 EXAMINER QUESTIONS)
================================================================================

Q1: "Your title claims you can probe 'any AI system'. What if the target is an 
     image model or a traditional Random Forest that does not have an MCP interface?"
A1: "In modern production environments, AI models are rarely exposed as raw neural 
     net weights; they are wrapped in microservice APIs or agentic tool harnesses. 
     AI-GRID targets MCP-compliant systems. For non-MCP models (such as image 
     classifiers or tabular tree ensembles), we employ the 'MCP Adapter Pattern'—a 
     lightweight Python wrapper that exposes the model's inference function as an MCP 
     tool. This enables AI-GRID to conduct standardized black-box probing regardless 
     of the underlying machine learning framework."

Q2: "Where do these scores (e.g., Security = 58.9, Fairness = 49.4) come from? 
     Are they arbitrary numbers?"
A2: "No. Every score is derived from deterministic, empirical formulas based on 
     probe observations and benchmark datasets. Fairness is calculated using 
     demographic parity and disparate impact (DIR/SPD) on the UCI German Credit 
     benchmark. Security is evaluated through input schema validation, dangerous 
     primitive detection, error trace suppression, and compound CVE/EPSS risk."

Q3: "Can AI-GRID legally certify that an AI system is compliant with the EU AI Act?"
A3: "No software tool can legally certify an AI system, as EU AI Act conformity 
     assessment requires human audits, organizational documentation, and review by a 
     Notified Body. AI-GRID acts as an 'Automated Technical Pre-Audit and Evidence 
     Harvester.' It collects empirical evidence against technical requirements in 
     Articles 9, 10, 13, 14, and 15, identifying vulnerabilities before formal audit."

Q4: "Why did you use the Model Context Protocol (MCP) instead of a simple REST API?"
A4: "A REST API tests simple text-in, text-out behavior. MCP standardizes the 
     interaction boundary between AI agents and external tools. In autonomous and 
     agentic systems, the primary attack surface lies in how agents declare tools, 
     pass arguments, and handle tool responses. MCP allows AI-GRID to probe tool 
     poisoning, argument manipulation, privilege escalation, and human-in-the-loop 
     safeguards at the protocol level."

Q5: "Explain the Bottleneck Principle in your scoring algorithm."
A5: "In high-stakes AI governance, a simple arithmetic average is dangerously misleading. 
     For example, a medical diagnostic AI with 95% transparency and 95% privacy would 
     average 95%, masking a 20% score in safety that could kill a patient. Inspired by 
     the Risk-Based AI Assurance Framework (RBAAF), AI-GRID uses a bottleneck-gated 
     formula: AAS = 0.6 * Weighted_Average + 0.4 * Min(Dimension_Scores). The overall 
     score is mathematically constrained by the system's weakest link."

Q6: "How does your Explainability probe verify TreeSHAP?"
A6: "Rather than simply checking if text was generated, AI-GRID validates the SHAP 
     Efficiency Axiom: the sum of local feature attributions must equal the difference 
     between the model prediction and the base expected value. It also checks that 
     relative feature contributions and prediction confidence are explicitly declared 
     in the tool payload."

Q7: "What is the primary novel research contribution of AI-GRID?"
A7: "AI-GRID connects runtime protocol probing of MCP-connected agents with evidence-based 
     governance assessment, solving two major industry problems:
     1. Multi-Tier Evidence Confidence Weighting: distinguishing runtime wire-level 
        tests (Tier T1, weight 1.0) from declared schemas (Tier T2, weight 0.8) and 
        self-reported questionnaires (Tier T3, weight 0.6).
     2. Attestation Discrepancy Index (ADI): mathematically quantifying developer 
        optimism bias by measuring the delta between self-reported governance claims 
        and runtime protocol reality (Delta = S_self - S_probe)."

Q8: "How does the What-If Simulation Engine work?"
A8: "The simulation engine models how risk changes when the same AI is redeployed in 
     different contexts. Deploying an agricultural model in healthcare increases risk 
     due to higher severity of harm (multiplier = 1.5x). Scaling from 5,000 to 500,000 
     users increases exposure. The engine computes:
     Adjusted_Risk = (100 - Base_AAS) * Domain_Multiplier * Scale_Factor."

Q9: "How do your remediation recommendations work?"
A9: "AI-GRID is a decision-support system, not just a scanner. For every identified 
     vulnerability, it maps the finding to specific EU AI Act articles and NIST 
     subcategories, explains the root cause, and provides step-by-step technical 
     remediation guidance, including code snippets and configuration requirements."

Q10: "What are the limitations of AI-GRID?"
A10: "First, AI-GRID conducts black-box behavioral probing; it does not inspect internal 
      neural network weights. Second, it tests technical and operational controls but 
      cannot evaluate organizational governance policies (such as whistleblower 
      protections). Third, counterfactual generation currently relies on predefined 
      domain test vectors rather than dynamically generated generative adversarial cases."
